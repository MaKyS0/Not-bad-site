# Rapid mod — design spec for walls, drills, mech units

This is the source of truth for the content added on top of the turrets. Numbers and visual
design here are final; the generator code (`tools/`) implements them.

Everything stays **pure hjson + PNG** (no Java, no JS) so the mod keeps working on iOS.
Everything under `content/`, `sprites/`, `bundles/` is generated by `python3 tools/gen.py`.

## 0. Format facts (checked against the v146 game classes — do not guess beyond these)

- Mod name is `rapid-turret`; content name = `rapid-turret-<file name>`; sprite region name =
  `rapid-turret-<png name>`. References inside hjson may use the short name (`pellet`, `rapid-wall-1`).
- hjson: one value per line. A quoteless string runs to the end of the line, so arrays of
  `item/amount` MUST be one entry per line. Colours are written quoted: `"ffbe50"`.
- `research: <parent>` or `research: { parent: <name>, requirements: [ ... ] }` puts content in the tech tree.
- Block classes used: `Wall`, `Drill`, `UnitFactory`. Unit `type: mech`.
- `Wall` fields: `lightningChance, lightningDamage, lightningLength, lightningColor, chanceDeflect, flashHit`
  (+ `Block`: `health, size, armor, absorbLasers, insulated, requirements, category`).
- `Drill` fields: `tier, drillTime, liquidBoostIntensity, rotateSpeed, drawMineItem, drawRim, drawSpinSprite,
  hardnessDrillMultiplier, warmupSpeed`. Regions: `<name>` (base), `<name>-rotator`, `<name>-top`, `<name>-item`
  (drawn last, tinted with the ore colour).
- `consumes: { power: <float>, liquid: { liquid: water, amount: <float>, optional: true, booster: true } }`.
- `UnitFactory`: `plans: [ { unit: <name>, time: <ticks>, requirements: [ ... ] } ]`.
  Regions: `<name>`, `<name>-out` (drawn rotated, opening on the RIGHT side at rotation 0), `<name>-top`
  (drawn over the unit under construction), `<name>-in`. Ship all four.
- Mech unit regions: `<name>` (body), `<name>-cell` (team colour, drawn tinted), `<name>-base` (hips),
  `<name>-leg` (ONE leg, same canvas as the body, placed on the right half; the game mirrors it).
- Weapon: `name: <x>` → sprite `<x>.png` (the parser prefixes the mod name). Weapon sprite is centred on
  its mount point and points UP. Weapon fields used: `name, x, y, top, mirror, alternate, reload, recoil,
  shootY, inaccuracy, ejectEffect, bullet`.
- The game generates the dark outline for **turrets, units and weapons** itself. Those sprites are drawn
  WITHOUT an outline and keep a transparent margin (5px turrets, 4px units/weapons).
  Walls, drills and the factory are full-tile blocks: no outline is generated, they fill the whole canvas.
- Sprites: 32px per tile, 4px = 1 world unit. Units and guns face UP.
- Effects known to exist: `casing1 casing2 casing3 shootSmall shootBig shootSmallSmoke shootBigSmoke
  hitBulletSmall hitBulletBig`.

## 1. Shared visual language

Pixel art, hard edges, no anti-aliasing, left/right mirror symmetry, light from the top
(top edge of every plate 1px lighter, bottom edge 2–3px darker) — exactly what `Art.plate` in
`tools/gen.py` does. Reuse that helper.

Tier colours (same as the turrets):

| Tier | accent | metal (light plate) | steel (dark plate) |
|------|--------|---------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | (255,190,80) orange | (152,154,164) | (110,112,128) |
| 2 | (90,215,255) cyan   | (146,158,176) | (98,108,132)  |
| 3 | (120,255,150) green | (150,164,156) | (96,114,110)  |
| 4 | (255,110,215) pink  | (160,152,172) | (104,94,124)  |
| 5 | (255,84,60) red     | (168,150,148) | (92,76,80)    |

**Emblem** — the mod's mark, used on walls, drill frames and the forge: two stacked chevrons
pointing up (`^` over `^`), drawn in the accent colour, 1px stroke on 32px art, 2px on 64px+.
Each chevron is `w` wide and `w/2` tall; the second sits `w/2 + 1` below the first.

## 2. Walls — 10 blocks

`rapid-wall-N` (1x1) and `rapid-wall-N-large` (2x2), N = 1..5. `type: Wall`, `category: defense`.
Large = 4x health and 4x cost of the small one.

| N | health/tile | armor | cost per tile | extra |
|---|-------------|-------|---------------|-------|
| 1 | 420  | 0 | copper 6, graphite 2 | — |
| 2 | 640  | 0 | titanium 6, silicon 3 | — |
| 3 | 920  | 2 | thorium 6, titanium 4 | — |
| 4 | 1250 | 4 | plastanium 5, thorium 5 | `absorbLasers: true`, `insulated: true` |
| 5 | 1700 | 6 | surge-alloy 6, phase-fabric 3 | `lightningChance: 0.05`, `lightningDamage: 25`, `lightningLength: 17`, `lightningColor: accent`, `chanceDeflect: 10`, `flashHit: true` |

Research: `rapid-wall-1` ← `copper-wall`; `rapid-wall-N` ← `rapid-wall-(N-1)`;
`rapid-wall-N-large` ← `rapid-wall-N`.

**Sprite (32px small / 64px large), fills the whole canvas:**
1. Whole tile = steel, darkened 0.7 on the outermost 1px ring (2px on large) so neighbouring walls read as separate plates.
2. Inset plate (inset 2px small / 4px large) in metal with the standard bevel; corners chamfered by 2px / 4px.
3. Inner recessed panel (inset 8px small / 16px large) in steel, bevel inverted (dark top edge, light bottom edge) so it reads as sunk in.
4. Emblem centred in the panel: `w` = 8px small, 16px large.
5. Rivets: 1px (2px large) dots in metal×0.62 at the four corners of the metal plate, 4px (8px) in from the edge.
6. Tier 4 adds a 1px (2px) accent line along the top and bottom inner edge of the panel (energy shielding look).
   Tier 5 adds that line on all four sides of the panel.
7. Large walls only: 1px dark seam cross through the metal plate (not through the panel), so it looks like four armour tiles.

`rapid-drill-N`. `type: Drill`, `category: production`.

| 3 | 3 | 4 | 230 | 1.5 | 0.09 | 4.5 | copper 60, graphite 50, silicon 45, titanium 35 |

**Sprites (S = size × 32), four layers on the same canvas:**

- **Base `rapid-drill-N.png`** (opaque, full tile): steel housing with a 1px (2px for S ≥ 128) darker outer ring;
  chamfered metal frame plate inset 2px; a round **well** in the centre, radius 0.40·S, filled with
  steel×0.45, with a 2px steel×0.62 lip around it; four corner blocks keep the metal frame. On each corner block:
  one rivet and a short accent tick (L-shape for N ≥ 3). Emblem (`w` = S/6) on the bottom-centre frame edge only if
  the frame there is at least `w/2 + 6` px tall — otherwise skip it.
- **Rotator `-rotator.png`** (transparent background): rotor centred in the canvas, radius 0.36·S.
  N blades by tier: 1→3, 2→4, 3→4, 4→6, 5→6. Each blade is a tapered wedge (wide at the hub, about 0.10·S at the tip
  ... i.e. narrow at the tip, 0.16·S wide at the hub) in metal with a lighter leading edge and a darker trailing edge,
  plus a small accent tip (last 2–4px). Must look the same after rotating by 360°/blades.
- **Top `-top.png`** (transparent background): octagonal hub, radius 0.15·S, metal with bevel, a dark round
  socket in its centre (radius 0.07·S, steel×0.45). For N ≥ 3 add a thin steel ring (2px) at radius 0.20·S around the hub.
- **Item `-item.png`** (transparent background): pure white diamond (rotated square), half-diagonal 0.06·S,
  centred — it sits inside the socket and the game tints it with the ore colour.

T3: `canDrown: false`.

Weapon mount (world units; `x` sideways, `y` forward) = half the body width + 1, i.e. `x`: 4.75, 5.75, 7.5, 12, 15.5;
`y`: 0.5, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2; `shootY` = half the gun sprite height / 4 − 1.

# Part 2 — conveyors and air units

Same rules as above (pure hjson + PNG, generated by `python3 tools/gen.py`, same visual language and tier colours).

## 6. Format facts for this part (checked against the v146 sources)

- `Conveyor` fields: `speed`, `displayedSpeed` (+ `health`, `requirements`, `category: distribution`).
  Regions: `<name>-<v>-<f>` for shape `v` = 0..4 and animation frame `f` = 0..3 — **20 PNGs of 32×32 per conveyor**.
  The game rotates/flips them itself; all are drawn for a belt whose OUTPUT is on the RIGHT edge:
  - `0` straight: input from the left.
  - `1` curve: input from the TOP edge, output right.
  - `2` input from the left AND the BOTTOM edge.
  - `3` input from the left, top AND bottom.
  - `4` input from the top AND bottom edges, nothing from the left.
  Frames advance in the direction of travel; the block icon is `<name>-0-0`.
- Flying unit: `type: flying` plus `flying: true` (the flag is not implied by the type). Regions: `<name>` (body) and
  `<name>-cell` only — no base, no legs. Engine flames are drawn by the game at `engineOffset` world units behind the
  centre with radius `engineSize`. Other fields used: `speed, accel, drag, rotateSpeed, health, armor, hitSize,
  lowAltitude, circleTarget, engineOffset, engineSize, weapons`.
- A weapon whose sprite does not exist is simply not drawn (the gun is then painted into the body sprite).
  A weapon with a sprite may set `rotate: true` and `rotateSpeed` to become a turning turret; it is drawn on top of the body.

## 7. Conveyors — 5 blocks

`rapid-conveyor-N`, `type: Conveyor`, `category: distribution`.

| N | speed | displayedSpeed | health | cost per tile |
|---|-------|----------------|--------|---------------|
| 1 | 0.05  | 7  | 50  | copper 1, graphite 1 |
| 2 | 0.085 | 12 | 70  | copper 1, lead 1, titanium 1, silicon 1 |
| 3 | 0.115 | 16 | 100 | titanium 1, silicon 1, thorium 1 |
| 4 | 0.15  | 21 | 140 | plastanium 1, thorium 1, silicon 1 |
| 5 | 0.19  | 26 | 190 | plastanium 1, surge-alloy 1, phase-fabric 1 |

Research: `rapid-conveyor-1` ← `conveyor`, then each from the previous tier.

**Sprites (32×32, coordinates x right, y down, origin top-left):**

Parts:
- **Belt**: steel×0.5, fills everything that is not rail, nub or cut corner.
- **Rail**: 4px thick strip of metal; its outermost row/column is metal×0.7, the row/column next to the belt is metal×1.2.
  Every rail carries static accent segments: 6px long, 2px thick (the two middle rows of the rail), one per 16px,
  starting 5px from the rail's start. Rails never animate.
- **Nub**: a 4×4 metal square in a corner (outer row and column metal×0.7).
- **Arrow**: a chevron 5px long and 9px across, 2px stroke, colour = steel×0.9 mixed toward the accent by
  0.25 / 0.35 / 0.5 / 0.65 / 0.8 for tiers 1..5. Arrows are the only thing that animates: each frame moves every arrow
  4px further along its lane; arrows repeat every 16px along a lane, so frame 4 would equal frame 0.
  An arrow is clipped to the belt (never drawn over rails or nubs).

Shapes:
- **0 straight**: rails along the whole top (y 0–3) and bottom (y 28–31). One lane left→right along y = 16; arrows point right,
  arrow tips at x = 6 + 4f and x = 22 + 4f (wrap around at 32 so the pattern is seamless between tiles).
- **1 curve**: rail along the whole LEFT edge (x 0–3) and the whole BOTTOM edge (y 28–31); the bottom-left corner is cut:
  the triangle below the line from (0,22) to (9,31) is fully transparent and the rail follows that diagonal (4px thick).
  Nub in the top-right corner. Lane: down the column x = 16 from the top edge to y = 16, then right along y = 16 to the right
  edge (total 32px, two arrows 16px apart, offset 4f). Arrows on the vertical leg point down, on the horizontal leg point right.
- **2**: rail along the whole top only. Nubs in the bottom-left and bottom-right corners. Main lane exactly as in shape 0.
  Side lane: up the column x = 16 from the bottom edge to y = 22; arrows point up; one arrow, tip at y = 30 − 4f
  (hidden once it would pass y = 22).
- **3**: no rails; nubs in all four corners. Main lane as in shape 0, the side lane of shape 2, and its vertical mirror from the top
  (arrow pointing down, tip at y = 1 + 4f, hidden once it would pass y = 9).
- **4**: rail along the whole LEFT edge. Nubs in the top-right and bottom-right corners. Two side lanes as in shape 3 (from top and
  bottom, meeting in the middle), and a right-pointing lane along y = 16 from x = 16 to the right edge (one arrow, tip at
  x = 18 + 4f, wrapping at 32 back to 16).

Tier flavour: tier 4 and 5 rails get a second accent segment per 16px (so the segments read as a dashed light strip).
Tier 5 additionally has a 1px accent line along the belt edge of each rail.

## 10. Format facts for this part (all checked with `javap -p [-c]` on the v146 `server.jar`)

- All classes used are in `mindustry.mod.ClassMap` (so `type:` resolves): `PowerTurret, ItemTurret, ShieldWall, MendProjector,
  LaserBoltBulletType, BasicBulletType, ShootSpread, ShootAlternate, ConsumeCoolant`.
- `PowerTurret` has ONE bullet: `shootType: { type: ..., ... }` (no `ammoTypes`). Power: `consumes: { power: <float per tick> }`
  (shown in game ×60). Its fire rate scales with power satisfaction. Draws exactly like `ItemTurret`.
- Turret regions (`DrawTurret`): `<name>` (gun, outlined by the game), `<name>-heat`, `<name>-base`. Nothing else is shipped.
- A turret calls `shoot()` at most once per tick, so `reload: 1` = 60 volleys/s is the ceiling; more comes only from `shoot.shots`.
- `Turret.shootEffect/smokeEffect` are null by default → the bullet's `shootEffect/smokeEffect` are used, drawn in the bullet's `hitColor`.
  `Turret.consumeAmmoOnce` is true by default: a `ShootSpread` volley of N pellets uses ONE ammo unit.
- `ShootSpread`: `shots`, `spread` = degrees BETWEEN neighbouring pellets (fan = (shots−1)·spread, centred on the aim).
- `LaserBoltBulletType` (extends `BasicBulletType`): `width`, `height`, `backColor`, `frontColor`. Its defaults are green
  (`hitLaser`), so `hitEffect`, `despawnEffect`, `smokeEffect` are overridden below.
- `ShieldWall` (extends `Wall`): `shieldHealth, breakCooldown (ticks), regenSpeed (shield points per tick), glowColor, glowMag, glowScl`.
  Region `<name>-glow` (drawn additively in `glowColor` while the shield is up). Damage hits the shield first; at 0 the shield is off
  for `breakCooldown`. The shield is also off while the block has no power. The shield itself is drawn by the game (team-coloured square).
  `conductivePower: true` (a `Block` field) makes touching power consumers share one power graph.
- `MendProjector` (NOT a `Wall`): `reload, range, healPercent, phaseBoost, phaseRangeBoost, baseColor, phaseColor`. Regions `<name>`,
  `<name>-top` (drawn tinted `baseColor`, pulsing alpha ≤ 0.5). Every `reload` ticks it heals each damaged friendly building whose
  centre is within `range + that building's size·4` world units — itself included — by `healPercent` % of that building's max health.
  **With no consumers its "optional boost" counts as always active**, i.e. `phaseBoost` and `phaseRangeBoost` are always added → both
  MUST be set to 0. `Wall`'s constructor defaults are copied by hand: `group: walls`, `priority: -2` (a float in this build),
  `buildCostMultiplier: 6`, `crushDamageMultiplier: 5`, `canOverdrive: false`.
- A regenerating wall as a true `Wall` subclass does not exist in v146 (no heal field on `Wall`/`Block`); `RegenProjector` needs a
  drawer and is Erekir-only, so `MendProjector` with a 2-unit range is the pure-hjson way.
- Names verified to exist — `Fx`: `none, hitLaserColor, shootSmallColor, shootBigColor, shootSmall, shootBig, shootSmallSmoke,
  shootBigSmoke, hitBulletSmall, hitBulletBig, casing2, casing3`. `Sounds`: `lasershoot, shotgun`. Items: `scrap, lead, graphite, thorium …`.
- Colours with alpha are 8 hex digits, quoted: `"ffbe5080"`.

## 19. Format facts for this part (checked with `javap -p -c -constants` on the v146 `server.jar` and the v146 sources)

- Unit type string is **`type: naval`**. `ContentParser` maps it to `UnitWaterMove::create` (bootstrap method #11 of the
  `flying/mech/legs/naval/…` switch; any other string throws "Invalid unit type").
- **Do NOT write `naval: true`, `canDrown` or `omniMovement`.** `UnitType.init()` sets them itself when the constructed unit is a
  `WaterMovec`: `naval = true`, `canDrown = false`, `omniMovement = false`, adds the `wet` immunity and the naval path cost.
  (This is the opposite of the air units, where `flying: true` had to be written.) The source comment on `naval` says "do NOT assign this manually".
- Regions loaded and drawn for a naval unit: `<name>` (body) and `<name>-cell` only. `-base`/`-leg` are drawn only for `Mechc`. No engine flames.
- Wave trail (drawn by `UnitWaterMove` under the unit, coloured from the water floor, only while on a liquid floor): two trails that start
  at `(±waveTrailX, waveTrailY)` world units from the centre (`waveTrailY` negative = behind), `trailLength` points long (0 = none, the
  default), width `trailScl`. All four are `UnitType` fields.
- `faceTarget: false` (a `UnitType` field, default true) — every vanilla ship sets it; the hull keeps its heading and only the turrets turn.
  Therefore **every naval weapon here has a sprite and `rotate: true`**.
- Speed on water: a `UnitWaterMove` moves at `speed × 1.3` on deep water and `speed × 1` on shallow water. It cannot leave liquid floors.
- `Weapon.mirror` defaults to **true** → centreline turrets (`x: 0`) MUST write `mirror: false`. A non-mirrored weapon fires once per `reload`.
- Multi-barrel centreline turret: `shoot: { type: ShootAlternate, barrels: <n>, spread: <world units between barrel centres> }`
  (`ShootAlternate.barrels`, `.spread`; same class the Pulse turrets use). Shot `i` leaves at `(i mod n − (n−1)/2) · spread` sideways.
- Independent anti-air mount: `controllable: false` + `autoTarget: true` (`Weapon` fields; `autoTarget` only works when `controllable` is false).
  Such a weapon picks its own target with `findTarget(…, bullet.collidesAir, bullet.collidesGround)`, so it follows the bullet's flags.
- `FlakBulletType` (in `ClassMap`, extends `BasicBulletType`): its constructor sets `collidesGround = false` → flak mounts target and hit
  **air only**. Fields used: `damage, speed, lifetime, splashDamage, splashDamageRadius, width, height, frontColor, backColor, hitEffect`
  (+ its own `explodeRange`, default 30, left alone).
- Vanilla `naval-factory` is a plain `UnitFactory` with exactly one extra field: **`floating: true`** (`Block.floating`). `Build.validPlace`:
  a tile on deep water is refused unless `floating` (or `requiresWater`/`placeableLiquid`) is set, and the block must touch at least one
  non-deep tile (inside it or on its edge ring) — so "on shallow water or on deep water next to the shore". `UnitFactory` itself has no water logic.
  Vanilla does NOT forbid building it on land. `requiresWater: true` exists (`Block.requiresWater`: every covered tile must have
  `liquidDrop == water`) but is a pump rule, is not what vanilla uses, and is **not used** here.
- A finished ship leaves the factory only if the tile one world unit in front of the exit is a liquid floor and not solid
  (`UnitPayload.dump` → `EntityCollisions.waterSolid`). The exit side must face water; otherwise the unit stays inside (vanilla behaviour).
- Research parent block name is `naval-factory` (`Blocks.navalFactory = new UnitFactory("naval-factory")`).
- Names verified to exist — `Fx`: `flakExplosion, flakExplosionBig, hitBulletSmall, hitBulletBig, casing1, casing2, casing3`.
  `Sounds`: `shootSnap`. `UnitType`: `faceTarget, trailLength, waveTrailX, waveTrailY, trailScl, accel, drag, rotateSpeed, hitSize, armor`.
  `Weapon`: `name, x, y, top, mirror, alternate, rotate, rotateSpeed, controllable, autoTarget, reload, recoil, shootY, inaccuracy,
  ejectEffect, shootSound, shoot, bullet`.
- Not possible in pure hjson and therefore not used: abilities with constructor arguments (`ShieldRegenFieldAbility` has no no-arg
  constructor), forbidding land placement of the factory without `requiresWater`.
- Vanilla ship sprites are not square (risso 70×78 … omura 264×351) — non-square unit canvases are fine (`Art(W, H)`).

## 25. Performance pass, level 3 (gameplay changes, visuals kept)

Chosen by the player from the Android performance analysis. Bullet trails, smoke and casings are kept on purpose.
- **Regen walls removed from the game** (sections 14, 15.4 are obsolete): every one was a `MendProjector` that updates and draws each
  frame; hundreds of them dropped a host to 8 FPS. Saves that contain them lose those blocks on load.
- Rapid VI: `shots: 1` instead of 2 at `reload: 1`, all ammo damage ×2 (60 bullets/s instead of 120, same DPS);
  surge ammo `lightning: 1` with `lightningDamage = 2·damage/3` (same lightning damage per hit).
- Silicon ammo (Rapid I–VI): no homing (`homingPower`/`homingRange` searched for a target every tick per bullet); damage +10 %.
- Buckshot IV: 5 pellets (spread 9°, same 36° fan) instead of 7, pellet damage ×7/5; the gun sprite has 5 bores.
- T4–T5 units (barrage, storm, tempest, hurricane, frigate, dreadnought): main guns `reload` at least 7, damage scaled to keep DPS.
- Drills: `drawSpinSprite: false` (the default draws the rotor twice, cross-faded, and assumes 90° symmetry — wrong for 3/6 blades),
  `updateEffectChance: 0` (no dust particles; also disables the per-item mine effect, which shares that chance in v146 `Drill`).
  The `-rotator`, `-top` and `-item` layers are cropped to a centred square around their content (T5: 120, 66, 20 px instead of
  160), so far less transparent area is drawn every frame; the composite is pixel-identical.

## 26. Format facts for this part (checked with `javap -p [-c -constants]` on the v146 `server.jar` and the v146 sources)

**Legged units**
- Unit type string is **`type: legs`** → `LegsUnit::create` (`ContentParser.unitType`, the same switch as `mech`/`naval`).
- `UnitType.init()` sets `allowLegStep = true` itself for a `Legsc` unit (walks over buildings; only static walls and solid floors with
  no block are `legSolid`) and picks the legs path cost. **Do not write `allowLegStep`.** Do write `hovering: true` (every vanilla legged
  unit does: the unit ignores the floor under it and cannot drown) — it is NOT implied.
- Regions loaded by `UnitType.load()`: `<name>` (body), `<name>-cell`, `<name>-leg`, `<name>-leg-base` (falls back to `-leg`),
  `<name>-joint`, `<name>-foot`, `<name>-joint-base`, `<name>-base`. Only body and `-leg` are needed; every other one is drawn only
  `if(region.found())`. This part ships body, cell, `-leg`, `-leg-base`, `-joint` and (T4–T5 only) `-foot`; **no** `-joint-base`, **no** `-base`.
- The game outlines body, `-leg`, `-leg-base`, `-joint`, `-foot` and weapons itself (radius 3) → all are drawn WITHOUT outline.
- How legs are drawn (`UnitType.drawLegs`), per leg, under the body:
  - `-leg` is **stretched** along the line leg root → joint, `-leg-base` along joint → foot point (`Lines.line(region, …)`): the sprite's
    LEFT edge is the inner end, its RIGHT edge the outer end, and the drawn thickness is the sprite HEIGHT (px / 4 world units).
    The length is whatever the leg currently spans (about `legLength / 2` world units per segment at rest, up to 1.75× when stretched).
  - Legs on one side are drawn with a negative stroke, i.e. **mirrored top ↔ bottom** → leg strips must be symmetric about their
    horizontal centre line (no top-light bevel on them).
  - `-joint` is drawn unrotated at the joint. `-foot` is drawn at the foot point, rotated so that the sprite's **+x (right) side points
    away from the body**; when a foot exists the game also draws a soft shadow blob per foot.
- Leg `i` of `n` leaves the body at angle `360/n · (i + 0.5)`° from the walking direction, from a root `legBaseOffset` world units from
  the centre; the foot rests `legLength · legLengthScl` further out. With the `legBaseOffset` values of section 27 every root lies under the body sprite, so no sockets are drawn.
- `UnitType` fields used (all verified): `legCount, legLength, legSpeed, legForwardScl, legBaseOffset, legMoveSpace, legPairOffset,
  legLengthScl, legSplashDamage, legSplashRange, hovering, groundLayer, shadowElevation, stepShake, rippleScale, drownTimeMultiplier,
  drag, targetAir`. `groundLayer` is a float; `Layer.legUnit` = 75 (mechs are at 60) → write the number.
- `legGroupSize` (default 2), `legExtension` (0), `legMaxLength` (1.75), `lockLegBase`, `legContinuousMove`, `flipBackLegs` stay at their defaults.

**Artillery**
- `ArtilleryBulletType` (in `ClassMap`, extends `BasicBulletType`). Its constructor sets `collides = false`, `collidesTiles = false`,
  `collidesAir = false`, `scaleLife = true`, `hitShake = 1`, `hitSound = explosion`, `hitEffect = flakExplosion`, `shootEffect = shootBig`,
  `trailEffect = artilleryTrail`, `shrinkX = 0.15`, `shrinkY = 0.63`. A shell therefore touches nothing in flight and deals only
  `splashDamage` within `splashDamageRadius` where it lands; `damage` is never applied (it is only shown in the stats).
- `scaleLife`: the shell's life is scaled so it lands on the aim point — turrets: `clamp(dst / (speed·lifetime), minRange/…, range/…)`,
  unit weapons: `clamp(dst / (speed·lifetime))`. **`speed × lifetime` must be ≥ the range and should exceed it by < 4**, otherwise shells fall short
  (vanilla calls `limitRange()` in Java for this; hjson cannot, so `lifetime` is written explicitly).
- `ArtilleryBulletType.update` spawns `trailEffect` every 3–5 ticks per shell (one effect entity each, living ~50 ticks). This part sets
  **`trailEffect: none`** and uses the bullet's built-in ribbon (`trailLength`, `trailWidth`, `trailColor`) like the other bullets of the mod.
- `Turret` fields used: `targetAir, targetGround, minRange, shootCone, velocityRnd, inaccuracy, recoil, shake, shootY, ammoPerShot,
  maxAmmo, ammoUseEffect, shootSound, heatColor, shoot` (+ `range, rotateSpeed, coolant` from `BaseTurret`/`ReloadTurret`).
  `Weapon` additionally has `shake`, `velocityRnd`, `shootCone`.
- A unit with `targetAir: false` never picks aircraft as its target (vanilla `fortress` does this for its artillery). A weapon with
  `controllable: false` + `autoTarget: true` still finds its own targets from its bullet's `collidesAir/collidesGround` (section 19) — this
  is how the walkers' flak works while the unit itself ignores aircraft.

**Power**
- `ConsumeGenerator`, `SolarGenerator`, `Battery` are in `ClassMap`. `PowerGenerator.powerProduction` is **per tick** (shown ×60).
- `ConsumeGenerator` fields: `itemDuration` (ticks one item lasts), `warmupSpeed`, `effectChance` (0.01), `generateEffect`, `consumeEffect`.
  Output = `powerProduction × efficiency × efficiencyMultiplier`, where the multiplier comes from a *filter* consumer:
  `itemFlammable` → the item's flammability (coal 1.0, spore pod 1.15, pyratite 1.4, blast compound 0.4), `itemRadioactive` → its
  radioactivity (thorium 1.0, phase fabric 0.6). A plain `item:` consumer has multiplier 1.
- Consumers the block parser accepts inside `consumes: { … }` (the `switch` in `ContentParser`, checked in bytecode): `item` (name, amount 1),
  `items`, `itemFlammable`, `itemRadioactive`, `itemCharged`, `itemExplosive`, `itemExplode`, `liquid`, `liquids`, `liquidFlammable`,
  `coolant`, `power`, `powerBuffered` (a number). `itemFlammable` / `itemRadioactive` must be OBJECTS:
  `itemFlammable: { minFlammability: 0.2 }`, `itemRadioactive: { minRadioactivity: 0.2 }` (those are the defaults, written out so the object is not empty).
- `drawer` CAN be set from hjson: `ContentParser` has a `DrawBlock` class parser — a string is a class name, an **array is shorthand for
  `DrawMulti`**, an object is `{ type: <DrawBlock class>, <fields> }` (`DrawDefault` when `type` is missing). `DrawMulti`, `DrawDefault`,
  `DrawWarmupRegion`, `DrawRegion`, `DrawGlowRegion`, `DrawPower` are all in `ClassMap` and have no-arg constructors.
  The default drawer of every `PowerGenerator` is `DrawDefault` (one `Draw.rect` of `<name>`), so a generator with only `<name>.png` needs no `drawer`.
- `DrawWarmupRegion`: region `<name>-top`, drawn tinted with `color` at alpha = warmup × (0.4 … 1.0 pulsing). Fields `color`, `sinMag`, `sinScl`.
- `ConsumeGenerator.init()` hard-codes `emitLight = true`, `lightRadius = 65·size` (only matters on maps with lighting).
- `SolarGenerator`: no consumers; `updateTile` is one multiplication (`solarMultiplier × light`). Output therefore follows the map rules.
- `Battery`: constructor sets `update = false` (it is never ticked) and `consumesPower/outputsPower`. Capacity: `consumes: { powerBuffered: <units> }`.
  Default drawer = `DrawMulti(DrawDefault, DrawPower, DrawRegion("-top"))`: `<name>` first, then — because no `<name>-power` region is
  shipped — a **filled square of side `8·size − 2` world units** (88 px of 96 for size 3) coloured `emptyLightColor → fullLightColor` by charge,
  then `<name>-top` over it. So `-top` carries the whole art with transparent windows and is REQUIRED. Icon = `<name>` + `<name>-top`.
- Research parents (block names from `Blocks.java`): `combustion-generator`, `solar-panel-large`, `battery-large`, `hail`, `ground-factory`.
- Names verified to exist — `Fx`: `none, flakExplosion, flakExplosionBig, blastExplosion, plasticExplosion, shootBig, shootBigSmoke,
  shootSmall, shootSmallSmoke, casing2, casing3, generatespark`. `Sounds`: `artillery, bang, explosion, smelter, shootSnap`.
  `StatusEffects`: `blasted`. `Block`: `ambientSound, ambientSoundVolume, liquidCapacity, itemCapacity`.
- Not used on purpose: `ConsumeItemExplode` (vanilla burners explode when fed blast compound — the Dynamos just burn it badly),
  `NuclearReactor`/`ImpactReactor` (heat and explosion logic every tick), `ThermalGenerator` (needs vents), `outputLiquid`, homing
  artillery (section 25), `makeFire`/incendiary shells (every fire is an entity).

## 34. Performance notes (why each addition respects section 25)

- **Walkers.** Update: `LegsComp.update` does one two-bone IK solve and one floor lookup per leg per tick — 4 or 6 legs, the same code vanilla
  runs for atrax/spiroct/arkyid. Draw: per leg 3 quads (`-leg`, `-leg-base`, `-joint`) for T1–T3 = 12–18 quads, and 5–6 (plus foot and its
  shadow) for T4–T5 = ≤ 36 quads, all from one atlas page, versus 4 quads for a mech. Kept down by: 6 legs instead of 8 on the goliath, no
  `-joint-base`, no `-base`, no feet on the three cheap tiers that appear in numbers. Weapons: mortars fire 1.5–3.75 shells/s per unit and a
  shell in flight does **no collision checks at all** (`collides = false`) — it is cheaper per frame than one Rapid bullet; its cost is one
  splash query when it lands. No `artilleryTrail` effects. Flak: 5–7.5 bullets/s per T3–T5 unit, the already-accepted naval flak.
  `legSplashDamage` (T4–T5 only) is one splash query per footfall, a few per second per unit, on units a player owns a handful of.
- **Mortar turrets.** 2–6 shells/s per turret (limit: 60). Shells skip collision entirely while flying; each landing is one
  `Damage.damage` over a radius of ≤ 39 world units (≤ 5 tiles). Ribbon trails only, no per-shell effect entities, no homing, no fragments, no fires.
- **Dynamos.** A `ConsumeGenerator` ticks every frame like any generator (a dozen float operations) and draws 2 quads (base + glow) when on
  screen; `generatespark` appears about 0.6 times a second per running block. This is exactly a vanilla combustion generator's cost, but the
  blocks are 4–65× denser, so a base needs FEWER ticking/synced buildings than with vanilla generators (`PowerGenerator` sets `sync = true`:
  each generator is included in the host's block snapshots — fewer generators also means less multiplayer traffic). They are not a
  mass-placed block like the removed Regen walls: one Dynamo III/IV feeds a whole turret cluster.
- **Solar Array.** `updateTile` is one multiplication, draw is 1 quad. 3 /tick per block means ~9 arrays where vanilla needs 20 large or 260
  small panels for one Pulse IV.
- **Capacitor.** `update = false` — the game never ticks it; 3 quads when on screen (base, charge square, top). The cheapest block in the mod.
- **Nest.** A fourth `UnitFactory`, identical in cost to the forge.

## 35. Theme and what was rejected

Theme: **the mod finally has its own factory floor.** Until now the mod only *spends* resources (every tier IV–VI block eats silicon,
plastanium and surge alloy by the thousand) and moves them on belts that vanilla junctions and bridges throttle to 11–14 items/s
while a Rapid Conveyor V carries 26. This release adds dense crafters (one building does the work of 2.5–6 vanilla ones), the
missing belt hardware, and the mod's first own material, **Rapid Steel**, with a full chain:
coal → *Rapid Press* → graphite → *Rapid Foundry* (+ titanium) → Rapid Steel → armour-piercing ammo and the frames of the
advanced blocks of this release.

Rejected for this release:
- **Liquids (pump, conduit, tank).** A conduit needs 5 autotile shapes × liquid/top/cap layers and every conduit tile ticks and
  draws a tinted liquid layer each frame — a mass-placed updating+drawing block, exactly what section 25 forbids. Vanilla conduits stay.
- **Support blocks (mender, overdrive, force projector).** Menders are the block that was removed in section 25; an overdrive dome
  multiplies turret fire rates past the 60 bullets/s cap of section 25. Not without a Java-side throttle.
- **Support / builder units.** Needs a fifth factory and unit line for a role vanilla Poly/Mega already fill; smaller gap than
  "the mod has no industry at all", and repair beams are per-frame drawn lasers on every unit.

## 36. Format facts for this part (checked with `javap -p [-c -constants]` on the v146 `server.jar` and the v146 sources)

**Item**
- A file `content/items/<name>.hjson` creates `mindustry.type.Item` named `rapid-turret-<name>` (`ContentParser`: `parser(ContentType.item, Item::new)`;
  no `type:` key). Items are loaded before blocks and units (`ContentType` order), so every block may reference the item.
- `Item` fields: `color, explosiveness, flammability, radioactivity, charge, hardness, cost, healthScaling, lowPriority, buildable, hidden`
  (+ `frames`, `transitionFrames`, `frameTime`, `hiddenOnPlanets` — not used). The default `color` is black, so `color` is REQUIRED.
- Icon: `UnlockableContent.loadIcon` looks up the region `<content name>` → a PNG **`sprites/items/rapid-steel.png`** (region
  `rapid-turret-rapid-steel`). Region names are one flat namespace: no other PNG of the mod may be called `rapid-steel.png`.
  Vanilla item sprites are 32×32 with a 1 px empty border; items are not outlined by the game.
- References: every `Content`-typed value (`ItemStack`, `ammoTypes` keys, `consumes.item`) is resolved as `rapid-turret-<x>` first and
  as plain `<x>` second (`ContentParser.internalRead` / `find`), so `rapid-steel` and `rapid-steel/10` work everywhere, including the
  `requirements` of blocks and `ammoTypes: { rapid-steel: { … } }`.
- `research: titanium` on an item puts it into the tech tree under the vanilla titanium node; the parser adds a `Produce` objective to
  every item node itself, and the node has no cost (`researchRequirements()` of an item is empty). In the campaign `Control.update` unlocks
  every item that is in the player's core, so the item unlocks the first time it is delivered. Bundle key: `item.rapid-turret-rapid-steel.name` / `.description`.
- `BulletType.pierceArmor` (boolean) exists: the bullet's damage ignores the target's `armor`.

**Crafters**
- `GenericCrafter` is in `ClassMap`. Fields: `outputItem` (an `ItemStack`, written `outputItem: graphite/3`), `craftTime` (ticks per
  craft), `craftEffect`, `updateEffect`, `updateEffectChance`, `warmupSpeed`, `drawer` (+ `Block`: `itemCapacity`, `liquidCapacity`,
  `ambientSound`, `ambientSoundVolume`). The constructor sets `update`, `solid`, `hasItems`, `sync = true`, `ambientSound = machine`,
  `ambientSoundVolume = 0.03`, `craftEffect = updateEffect = none`.
- Inputs: `consumes: { items: [ … ], power: <per tick>, liquid: { liquid: oil, amount: <per tick> } }`. `items` as an ARRAY is read as
  `ItemStack[]` (one `item/amount` per line). The consumers switch on `hasPower` / `hasLiquids` themselves.
- `GenericCrafterBuild.warmup()` returns the crafter's warm-up, so the drawer of the Dynamos works unchanged:
  `drawer: [ { type: DrawDefault }, { type: DrawWarmupRegion, color: … } ]` — `<name>` plus `<name>-top` tinted with `color` at
  alpha = warmup × (0.4 … 1.0). `DrawWarmupRegion` fields: `color`, `sinMag`, `sinScl`.
- Output path: `craft()` calls `offload(item)` once per produced item, which hands the item straight to a neighbour that accepts it;
  what is left in the block leaves through `dump()` once every `dumpTime` ticks. **`Block.dumpTime` is `protected final int = 5`
  — it cannot be set from hjson** — so a backed-up crafter drains at most 12 items/s. Every crafter here outputs ≤ 6 items/s.
- A crafter accepts an input item while it holds fewer than `itemCapacity` of it and stops crafting when it holds `itemCapacity` of the
  output: `itemCapacity` must be ≥ 2 × the largest per-craft amount (table values satisfy this).
- Names verified — `Fx`: `pulverizeMedium, smeltsmoke, formsmoke, none`; `Sounds`: `smelter, machine`; `Category`: `crafting, distribution, effect`.
- Vanilla tech nodes used as parents exist under these names: `graphite-press`, `silicon-smelter`, `plastanium-compressor`,
  `surge-smelter`, `junction`, `bridge-conveyor`, `phase-conveyor`, `unloader`, `vault`, item `titanium`.

**Logistics**
- `Junction`: fields `speed` (ticks an item waits inside, default 26) and `capacity` (items buffered per direction, default 6).
  Throughput per direction = `capacity / speed × 60` items/s (vanilla 13.8). One region `<name>`; never rotates. `update = true`.
- `BufferedItemBridge` (vanilla "bridge conveyor") extends `ItemBridge`; its constructor sets `hasPower = false`. Fields `speed` (ticks an
  item spends in the buffer, vanilla 74), `bufferCapacity` (vanilla 14). It hands over one item every 4 ticks at most (`timerAccept`,
  hard-coded) → throughput = `min(15, bufferCapacity / speed × 60)` items/s (vanilla 11.4).
- `ItemBridge` (vanilla "phase conveyor"): `hasPower = true` in the constructor; needs `consumes: { power: … }`. Fields: `range`
  (an **int, in TILES**, required — default 0), `transportTime` (ticks per item, default 2 → nominally 30 items/s), `pulse`, `fadeIn`,
  `arrowSpacing` (world units between arrows, default 4), `arrowOffset`, `arrowPeriod`, `arrowTimeScl`, `bridgeWidth` (6.5).
  `moveArrows` exists but is not read by the v146 draw code — do not write it.
- Bridge regions (all 32×32, all REQUIRED, `@Load` without fallback): `<name>`, `<name>-end`, `<name>-bridge`, `<name>-arrow`.
  How `ItemBridgeBuild.draw` uses them, per linked bridge and frame:
  - `-end` twice (source and target), rotated so that the sprite's **BOTTOM edge faces the span** (vanilla art sits in rows 24..31).
  - `-bridge` once, **stretched** from tile edge to tile edge (`Lines.line(region, …)`, stroke 6.5 world units = the full 32 px sprite
    height squeezed into 26 px): its columns are stretched along the span, so every column must be identical, and it is flipped
    depending on the direction, so it must be symmetric top ↔ bottom.
  - `-arrow` `(tiles between the ends) × 8 / arrowSpacing` times, pointing along the link; the sprite points **RIGHT**; alpha pulses.
  - Everything above is multiplied by the player's "bridge opacity" setting.
- `Unloader`: field `speed` = **ticks per item** (vanilla `60/11`); smaller is faster. Regions `<name>` and `<name>-center`
  (drawn over the block tinted with the selected item's colour, invisible when nothing is selected). Vanilla puts it in `Category.effect`.
- `StorageBlock`: constructor sets `update = false` (never ticked), `hasItems`, `solid`. Capacity = `itemCapacity`. `coreMerge` (default
  true): touching a core, it adds its capacity to the core. One region `<name>`; the optional `<name>-team` overlay is NOT shipped.
- Not used on purpose: `Sorter`, `Router`, `OverflowGate` (vanilla ones transfer instantly — a "Rapid" version could not be faster),
  `MassDriver` (per-shot bullets and effects), `DrawFlame`/`DrawArcSmelt` (2 filled circles / 25 line particles per crafter per frame;
  the one-quad `DrawWarmupRegion` is used instead), `AttributeCrafter`, liquid outputs.

## 43. Performance notes (why each addition respects section 25)

- **Item, ammo.** An item costs nothing per frame. `pierceArmor` is a flag read when a bullet hits; the steel round has no pierce, no homing,
  no lightning — it is the cheapest bullet the Rapid line fires. Bullets per second do not change.
- **Crafters.** `GenericCrafter.updateTile` is a few float operations per tick plus one `dump` attempt every 5 ticks — the vanilla smelter's
  cost — and the block draws 2 quads (base + glow) instead of the vanilla smelter's quad + two filled circles. `craftEffect` spawns only while
  the block is on screen, 1–1.5 times a second. `GenericCrafter` is `sync = true` (sent in the host's block snapshots), so the point of the
  line is the same as with the Dynamos: one Rapid Press replaces 6 presses, one Smelter 4, one Compressor 3, one Arc Furnace 2.5 —
  a silicon/plastanium/surge base needs a quarter to a half of the ticking, synced buildings, and proportionally fewer conveyor tiles
  between them. They are few per base, never mass-placed.
- **Junction.** The vanilla class with two numbers changed: the same 4-buffer loop per tick, 1 static quad, no effects. It is placed
  where a vanilla junction would be placed, one for one.
- **Bridges.** Update = the vanilla bridge code. Draw per linked bridge: base + 2 ends + 1 strip + arrows; with `arrowSpacing: 8` that is at
  most 6 arrows for the Rapid Bridge (vanilla bridge at full range: 4) and 15 for the Phase Bridge (vanilla phase conveyor at full range: 22).
  A full-length bridge replaces 6 / 15 conveyor tiles, each of which would tick and draw a belt plus up to 4 item quads — so long bridges
  lower the frame cost of a transport line. All bridge drawing obeys the player's bridge-opacity setting (0 = nothing but the base quad).
- **Unloader.** Same algorithm as vanilla, run every ~3 ticks instead of ~5.5 (an idle unloader polls every tick in vanilla too); it moves
  twice the items, so a base needs half as many. 2 quads.
- **Vault.** `update = false` — never ticked; 1 quad (no team overlay). One replaces 2.5 vanilla vaults.
- Nothing in this part adds a mass-placed block that both updates and draws animated layers every frame: the only blocks placed in
  numbers (junction, bridges) are vanilla classes drawing static sprites, and each stands where a vanilla block — or several — would stand.

## 44. Theme and decision record

Section 35 rejected liquids and overdrive on performance grounds. **The player overruled both** ("do the first and the third, I don't
care"), so this release ships them; menders stay rejected (they are the block removed in section 25, and nobody asked for them back).
The performance cost is written down honestly in section 52 instead of being designed away.

Theme: **the mod finally moves and speeds up what it consumes.** Dynamo II drinks water, Dynamo IV cryofluid, the Rapid Compressor oil,
every turret takes coolant — all through vanilla pipes whose real bottleneck (section 45) is the bridge conduit and the router. This part
adds dense pumps (one building = 5 vanilla ones), pipes and bridges that carry them, and an overdrive line with longer reach.

Still rejected:
- **Menders / mend projectors** — section 25.
- **A "Rapid Liquid Junction".** `LiquidJunction` has no tunable field: it holds nothing and forwards liquid to the far side in the same
  tick (`getLiquidDestination`). A Rapid version could only differ in health. The vanilla junction works with every block below and is
  what a Rapid Conduit turns into when two lines are dragged across each other (`junctionReplacement` default).
- **More than two conduit tiers.** Flow through a pipe is ≈ 0.55 × `liquidCapacity` per tick (section 45); tier I already carries more
  than any single pump. Five tiers would be five reskins of the same number.
- **Animated pump rotors, own conduit bottom layers, liquid-drawing bridges** — extra quads per frame for no information.

## 45. Format facts for this part (checked with `javap -p [-c]` on the v146 `server.jar` and the v146 sources)

All eight classes are in `ClassMap`: `Conduit`, `ArmoredConduit`, `LiquidRouter`, `LiquidBridge`, `LiquidJunction`, `Pump`,
`OverdriveProjector`, `DrawPumpLiquid`. `Category` has `liquid` and `effect`.

**How liquid moves (`Building.moveLiquid` / `dumpLiquid`, `Block.liquidCapacity` default 10, `liquidPressure` default 1)**
- Conduits and bridges push with `moveLiquid`: per tick `flow = clamp(own/ownCap × liquidPressure − next/nextCap) × ownCap`, limited by
  what they hold and by the free space of the target. Routers, tanks and pumps push with `dumpLiquid`: per neighbour and tick
  `(own/ownCap − next/nextCap) × ownCap / 2` (bridge exits: `/ 1`).
- So the throughput of a pipe is proportional to its **`liquidCapacity`**; there is no speed field. Estimates below come from a script that
  iterates exactly these two formulas over a chain with an always-empty sink (worst case of 40 fixed and 5 random update orders; they are
  model numbers, not in-game measurements): vanilla conduit (10) ≈ 330 /s, pulse conduit (16, pressure 1.025) ≈ 520 /s,
  **bridge conduit / phase conduit (10) ≈ 365 /s**, liquid router (20) ≈ 485 /s. The largest vanilla pump makes 119 /s, so vanilla pipes
  never limit one pump; they limit merged lines and anything bigger — which this part adds.
- `liquidPressure` > 1 only lets a pipe keep pushing into a fuller neighbour; it changes the numbers above by a few percent.

**`Conduit` / `ArmoredConduit`**
- Fields: `botColor` (Color, default `565656`), `padCorners`, `leaks`, `junctionReplacement`, `bridgeReplacement`, `rotBridgeReplacement`
  (+ `Block`: `liquidCapacity`, `liquidPressure`, `health`). The constructor sets `rotate`, `solid = false`, `floating`, `underBullets`,
  `conveyorPlacement`, `noUpdateDisabled`, `canOverdrive = false`; `ArmoredConduit` additionally `leaks = false` and only accepts liquid
  from conduits, junctions and from the block directly behind it.
- Regions (`@Load`): **`<name>-top-0` … `<name>-top-4`** (required, no fallback); `<name>-bottom-0..4` with
  **fallback `conduit-bottom-#`** (the vanilla sprites — pulse and plated conduit ship none either, so neither do we);
  `<name>-cap` (optional: drawn only `if(capRegion.found())`; vanilla ships one for the plated conduit only).
  There is no `<name>.png`. The icon is composed by the game from the vanilla region `conduit-bottom` + `<name>-top-0`.
- Shape index = the conveyor's (`Autotiler.buildBlending`, section 6): every sprite is drawn for **output on the RIGHT edge**;
  `0` straight (input left); `1` curve, input from the **TOP** edge (image row 0); `2` input left and **BOTTOM**; `3` left, top and bottom;
  `4` top and bottom, nothing from the left. Mirrored cases are drawn with `Draw.scl(1, −1)`; the block rotation turns the sprite. So no
  sprite may have a top-light bevel.
- Per tile and frame `ConduitBuild.draw` draws: bottom region tinted `botColor` → one 32×32 animated fluid frame of the game, tinted with the
  liquid colour at alpha = fill level, **covering the whole tile** → the top region. Everything of the tile that is not channel must
  therefore be opaque in the top sprite. Exception, shape 1 (`padCorners`): the fluid quad is cut to **columns 6..31, rows 0..25**.
- Vanilla bottom masks (what shows through an empty channel; the transparent pixels of our tops must lie on them): `-bottom-0` rows 8..23;
  `-bottom-1` columns 7..31 of rows 0..19, then columns `y − 12 .. 31` of rows `y` = 20..24; `-bottom-2/3/4` the full tile.
- Next to a neighbour whose sprite is not square the game also draws half of `-top-0` (left or right 16 columns) as a stub under that
  neighbour; anything drawn in `-top-0` should therefore repeat every 16 columns.
- The cap is drawn rotated like the conduit when the tile in front has no liquid block, and turned 180° when a straight conduit has nothing
  behind it: the art sits on the **RIGHT** edge.
- `bridgeReplacement: <block>` (a `Block` field, resolved by name with the mod prefix first; all blocks exist by then because field reading is
  deferred to `finishParsing`): dragging a conduit line over an obstacle inserts that bridge — only if it is an `ItemBridge` subclass and
  `unlockedNow()`; otherwise nothing is inserted.
- `updateTile`: one `moveLiquidForward` per tick while the conduit holds liquid, `sleep()` when empty.

**`LiquidRouter`** (also the tank) — field `liquidPadding` (world units). Regions: `<name>-bottom` (drawn first) and **`<name>` (drawn LAST,
it is the top layer with the window)**; icon = bottom + top. Between them `size²` fluid quads are drawn at alpha = fill level; with
`liquidPadding: p` the fluid is cut to the block minus `p` world units per side and tiles that fall outside are skipped — a size-3 block
with `liquidPadding: 4` draws **4 quads covering pixels 16..79** instead of 9. Constructor: `solid = true`, `canOverdrive = false`.
Accepts from every side, dumps to every neighbour. The `-liquid` / `-top` regions that `LiquidBlock` declares are not drawn by it.

**`LiquidBridge`** extends `ItemBridge` — everything of section 36 about bridge regions and drawing holds unchanged (`<name>`, `-end`,
`-bridge`, `-arrow`; no liquid is drawn on the span). Differences: `range` (int, tiles) as before; `transportTime` is not used (liquid moves with
`moveLiquid` once per tick); throughput follows `liquidCapacity`. The `ItemBridge` constructor sets `hasPower = true`, and vanilla
`bridge-conduit` sets it back: **an unpowered liquid bridge must write `hasPower: false`**. `canOverdrive = false` is set by the class.

**`Pump`** — fields `pumpAmount` (liquid **per tick per covered liquid tile**, default 0.2), `consumeTime`, `warmupSpeed`, `drawer`
(default `DrawMulti(DrawDefault, DrawPumpLiquid)` — do not write the key). Output per second on plain water =
`pumpAmount × size² × 60`; every floor tile contributes its `liquidMultiplier` (deep water 1.5, cryofluid pools 0.5, others 1).
A multi-tile pump may be placed when at least one covered tile has a liquid and no two covered tiles have different ones.
With a power consumer the output scales with satisfaction; without `consumes` it runs free. Regions actually drawn: `<name>` and
`<name>-liquid` (`Drawf.liquid`: tinted with the liquid colour, alpha = fill level, drawn at the region's own size, centred — so it may be a
cropped square). Icon = `<name>`. Pumps CAN be overdriven (`edelta`).

**`OverdriveProjector`**
- Fields: `reload` (ticks between pulses, 60), `range` (**world units**, 8 per tile; 80), `speedBoost` (1.5), `speedBoostPhase` (0.75, added
  while the booster item is supplied), `phaseRangeBoost` (20), `useTime` (ticks per item use, 400), `hasBoost` (true), `baseColor`, `phaseColor`.
  Constructor: `hasPower`, `hasItems`, `canOverdrive = false`, `emitLight`, `lightRadius = 50`.
- Every `reload` ticks it calls `applyBoost(realBoost, reload + 1)` on every building in range whose block has `canOverdrive`;
  `applyBoost` keeps the **maximum** — projectors never stack. `realBoost = (speedBoost + phaseHeat × speedBoostPhase) × efficiency`.
- Regions: `<name>` and `<name>-top` (tinted `baseColor` → `phaseColor`, alpha pulsing 0 … 0.5, drawn at its own size, centred). Besides
  that the game draws one square outline that contracts once every 100 ticks (≤ 8 line points).
- Optional booster: `consumes.items` written as an OBJECT is read as a `ConsumeItems` with its flags —
  `items: { items: [ phase-fabric/1 ], optional: true, booster: true }`. Written as an ARRAY it is a mandatory consumer; with
  `hasBoost: false` the block then does nothing without the items (the vanilla dome works like that). `consume()` is called every `useTime`.
- The stat line prints `(int)(speedBoost × 100 − 100)`; 1.6, 2.0 and 3.0 print as +60 / +100 / +200 % (checked in float arithmetic).
- **What a boost does to the mod's turrets.** `Turret.updateShooting` fires at most ONE volley per tick (`if(reloadCounter >= reload){ shoot();
  reloadCounter %= reload; }`, and `reloadCounter` is capped at `reload`). A turret with `shots: 1` therefore never exceeds 60 bullets/s
  whatever the boost: Rapid V–VI and Pulse IV (`reload: 1`) gain nothing; Rapid I–IV and Pulse I–III rise to at most 60 /s. Turrets firing
  several bullets per volley do scale (Buckshot, vanilla turrets). Units are never boosted.
- Not boosted (`canOverdrive = false`): walls and Aegis walls, batteries, power nodes, conduits, liquid routers/tanks/bridges, projectors.
  Boosted: turrets, drills, crafters, unit factories, conveyors, pumps — and generators, where it is harmful: `ConsumeGenerator` burns
  fuel by `delta()` but its output does not grow, so **an overdriven Dynamo only eats fuel faster**.

Vanilla names used as research parents (checked in `Blocks.java` / `SerpuloTechTree.java`): `conduit`, `liquid-router`, `bridge-conduit`,
`phase-conduit`, `liquid-tank`, `mechanical-pump`, `overdrive-projector`.

### 49.1 Conduits — `rapid-conduit-N-top-0 … -top-4.png` (32×32) and `rapid-conduit-2-cap.png`; N = 1 uses tier 1 colours, N = 2 tier 3

Orientation: output on the RIGHT edge; shape 1 enters at the TOP edge, shape 2 at the left and BOTTOM edges (section 45). The geometry is
the vanilla one — walls 8 px, channel 16 px — so the vanilla bottom sprites fit underneath.

**Channel `C_k`** (transparent in the top sprite), with `H` = rows 8..23, `V` = columns 8..23:
- `C_0` = `H`.
- `C_1` = (`V` ∩ rows 0..23) ∪ (`H` ∩ columns 8..31), minus the chamfer pixels `{20 ≤ y ≤ 23, 8 ≤ x ≤ y − 12}` (10 pixels, they are wall).

**Wall colour.** Every non-channel pixel is wall. Full walls per shape: 0 — top and bottom; 1 — left and bottom; 2 — top; 3 — none; 4 — left.
Distance to a wall's tile edge: top `y`, bottom `31 − y`, left `x`. Let `F` = the full walls whose distance is < 8 at this pixel.
- one wall in `F`: `d` = that distance, `s` = position along the wall (`x` for top/bottom, `y` for left), *straight*;
- two walls in `F` (shape 1, the bottom-left 8×8): `d` = the smaller distance, *corner*;
- none (a nub — the 8×8 block in a tile corner): `d = max(min(x, 31 − x), min(y, 31 − y))`, *corner*.

- N = 1 stripe: `acc` on straight pixels with `s` in 5..10 or 21..26 (dashes), steel elsewhere (so nubs and corners have no accent).
- N = 2 stripe: `acc` everywhere (a continuous line that turns the corners); additionally rivets: straight pixels with `d` in 5..6 and
  `s` in {7, 8, 23, 24} are metal × 0.62.

## 52. Performance notes (honest: what each addition costs)

Section 25 is knowingly relaxed for this part by the player's decision; nothing below is hidden by that.

- **Conduits.** Exactly the cost of the vanilla conduit they replace, one for one: while wet, one `moveLiquid` per tick; per frame 3 quads
  (bottom, fluid, top) — this is the mass-placed "updates and draws every frame" block that section 35 warned about. What was done to keep
  it no worse than vanilla: no extra layers (arrows are painted into the top, bottoms are the shared vanilla regions), an empty conduit
  sleeps, and the long-range bridges below replace 6 / 15 conduit tiles with 2 buildings.
- **Liquid router.** One `dumpLiquid` pass over ≤ 4 neighbours per tick; 3 quads. One per junction of pipes, as in vanilla.
- **Liquid bridges.** The item-bridge code path (section 43): base + 2 ends + 1 strip + arrows at `arrowSpacing: 8` (≤ 6 / ≤ 15 arrows,
  vanilla phase conduit at full range: 22); no fluid is drawn on the span. All of it obeys the bridge-opacity setting.
- **Tank.** One `dumpLiquid` pass per tick over its neighbours; 6 quads (bottom, 4 fluid tiles, top) where a vanilla tank draws 11.
  One replaces 2.8 vanilla tanks.
- **Pumps.** A few float operations and one `dumpLiquid` pass per tick; 2 quads (the `-liquid` layer is cropped to the well); no effects,
  no rotor. Pump III replaces 4.85 impulse pumps (each 2 quads and a tick) and the conduit tiles between them.
- **Overdrive projectors.** Own cost is small: once per 60 ticks one indexed range query plus one `applyBoost` call per building in range;
  per frame 2 quads, one outline of ≤ 8 points and one light. The real cost is what they speed up: every boosted building runs
  `speedBoost` times as much logic per second, and multi-pellet turrets fire `speedBoost` times as many bullets (Buckshot IV: 60 → 180
  pellets/s under Overdrive III). Single-shot turrets are capped by the engine at 60 bullets/s, and Rapid V–VI / Pulse IV do not change
  at all (section 45). Boosts never stack, so a second projector over the same turrets adds only its own small cost.
- Net effect on a base that uses the line as intended (few big pumps, bridges instead of long pipe runs, one projector over a turret
  cluster): fewer liquid buildings than the vanilla equivalent, more bullets per second from Buckshot and vanilla turrets in overdrive range.
